# Changelog

## 1.5.0 — live tools, auto filters and Streamlabs

- **Live tools** (dashboard → *Live*): a poll, goals, commands and Discord, on all your platforms at once.
- **Poll on every platform**: a question with 2 to 5 answers, from 15 s to 30 min. Viewers vote by typing 1, 2… in any chat, TikTok included; one vote per person, the first counts. End it early whenever you want, and optionally announce the question and the result in every live chat. Votes never show on the chat overlay.
- **Poll overlay** (OBS → Overlay → *Poll*, 600 × 400): a dark card with the question, numbered answers whose bars fill with their share of the votes, the time left as a line running down the top edge, and the icons of the platforms viewers can vote from. The leading answer lights up in the Tramevia gradient and every new vote makes its number bounce. At the end a light sweeps across the winner, its number turns gold and the other answers dim; the result stays 15 s, then the card leaves. *Test a poll* plays a sample poll in the preview and in OBS without counting anything. Two styles: *Bars*, or *Ring*, where the answers are coloured arcs of one glass ring, the time runs round it with a bright dot and shows in its centre, and at the end the winning arc flares while its number and share take the centre.
- **Follower and sub goals**: counted on Twitch, Kick, YouTube and TikTok together from the moment you set them (a gifted sub counts once per sub, YouTube members count as subs). Title, goal and count can be edited, and the count reset.
- **Goal overlay** (OBS → Overlay → *Goal*, 800 × 200): one bar per goal (subs, follows or both) with a medal, a white-hot head running along the bar, and the count. Every rise fills the bar, rolls the count up and pops a +n beside it. Reaching the goal fires sparks and a ring out of the head, the medal turns gold and the title rolls to *Goal reached!*. Nothing moves at rest. *Test progress* shows a rise for a few seconds. Two styles: *Bars*, or *Ring*, a glass ring gauge round the medal for each goal (side by side when both are shown) that flashes on every rise and throws sparks and a shock ring when the goal is reached. A colour of your choice: the Tramevia gradient, 11 suggested colours or any colour from the picker, which tints the bar or ring, the medal and the reached state. One icon per goal among 18 line icons (star, heart, new follower, trophy, crown, rocket, gamepad…).
- **Commands and reminders**: `!discord`, `!socials`… answered in the chat where they were typed (at most once every 30 s, `{user}` = the person's name), and up to 10 reminders sent during the stream, every 5 to 240 min, to every chat that accepts messages, only where someone wrote since the previous one. You pick which accounts answer. On YouTube each message costs 50 quota units (out of 10,000 a day).
- **Discord**: paste a channel webhook and Tramevia Dock announces your stream one minute after it starts, with your message (`{name}`, `{title}`, `{category}`; @everyone and role mentions work) and the link of every live channel, then posts the recap when it ends. *Send a test* checks the webhook, which is stored encrypted.
- **Stream recap**: duration, peak viewers, messages, people in chat, follows and subs per platform and the 5 most active chatters, live in the dashboard during the stream and kept for the last one. A stream back within 5 min counts as the same session.
- **Auto filters** (Settings → Auto filters): banned words and links, on every platform, with a list of allowed sites (youtube.com also covers www. and m.). A filtered message never reaches the chat overlay, shows struck through in the chat dock with the reason, and is deleted on the platform when it allows it, with an optional timeout (1 min to 24 h). You, your moderators and optionally VIPs and subscribers are never filtered. **Anti-spam** (one switch each, off by default): too many capitals, the same message 3 times within a minute, more than 10 emotes or emoji, and symbol floods (!!!!!, ░▒▓…).
- **Old streams no longer come back in the chat dock**: the history now clears itself when a stream starts (one of your channels goes from offline to live), and a *Clear history* button in the chat settings empties it on every dock and on the chat overlay. Nothing is deleted on the platforms.
- **Streamlabs Desktop guide**: the dock goes in the layout editor's Website element and the overlays are Browser Sources ([docs/en/streamlabs.md](docs/en/streamlabs.md)). Tested with Streamlabs Desktop 1.21.9, all ten alert styles included.
- **Supporting the project is open**: the *Support the project* button leads to [Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/tramevia) (from $5 a month), and members get a supporter code by e-mail within minutes that unlocks the eight supporter alert styles right away. These styles no longer have an unlock date: they stay for supporters, Card and Big text stay free. The "(Soon)" labels are gone.
- **Supporter styles download on their own**: the eight supporter styles are no longer in Tramevia Dock itself. Once a supporter code is active, Tramevia Dock downloads them from the Tramevia server (at start, then once a day, for an update or a revoked code) and keeps them in its data folder, so a Railway install keeps them too. Settings → About shows whether they are downloaded. Removing the code deletes them.
- **New Tramevia logo**: the 3D lavender T inside a glowing ring. The app icon and tab icon carry the ring, the login page, the welcome card and Settings → About show the 3D T, and the README, the "how it works" diagrams and the GitHub social preview are redone with the new mark.
- **A cleaner interface in the Tramevia colours**: night surfaces tinted violet, a lavender accent with dark text on buttons (and a matching light theme), the Manrope font built into the app (no request to Google), and the 3D T in the header. The overview splits your total viewers by platform, the LIVE badges are softer and their red dot blinks, the demo banner is a thin strip, the dock header is 8 px shorter, and rows of chips fade out at the edge instead of being cut. The compact chat filters now tell similar names apart: "tramevia" and "TrameviaTV" were both "tra", now they are "TRA" and "TV". The overlays seen on stream do not change.
- **Lighter pages**: text files are sent compressed (brotli or gzip), the font and images are cached for good, and the other files answer "not modified" when nothing changed. The 3D logo is a 3 KB WebP instead of a 25 KB PNG. Each overlay loads only the code and styles of what it shows (the alert styles stay out of the chat, viewers, poll and goal overlays), and the dashboard gets the live tools' settings with its state, so it opens in fewer round trips. First load on a hosted install, eight pages: 2,555 → 757 KB (about 3× lighter), ready 13 % sooner on average; dashboard 596 → 121 KB, chat overlay 455 → 68 KB, viewers overlay 455 → 77 KB, alerts overlay 455 → 144 KB.
  ![Tramevia Dock 1.5 performance: eight pages go from 2,555 to 757 KB on first load and are ready 13 % sooner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tramevia/Tramevia-Dock/main/docs/assets/perf-1.5-en.png)
- **Chat dock message box no longer opens huge** when the dock loads in a hidden panel (Streamlabs): its height is measured again once the panel has its size.

## 1.4.0 — alerts and viewers overlays

- **Support the project (Soon)**: a *Support the project (Soon)* button in the dashboard header and in Settings → About. Sponsoring on GitHub Sponsors, with a supporter code that unlocks new alert styles early, opens in 1.4.5.
- **Early access for new alert styles (Soon)**: Card and Big text are free; the eight other styles show a lock (Soon). They open to supporters in 1.4.5, then to everyone. Unlock date: Soon 😎.
- **Deploy on Railway**: a one-click button in the README and the cloud guide. You only fill in a password; Railway sets up the volume, the domain, the healthcheck and nightly updates.
- **Viewers overlay**: a fourth overlay (OBS → Overlay → *Viewers*) shows your total live viewers across Twitch, Kick, YouTube and TikTok as an orb of glowing particles. Every rise animates in green (+x), every drop in red (−x); one platform poll gives one net change, never a burst. Settings: theme, where the number sits (inside the orb, left or right), text size, platforms and accounts counted. A **Test a change** button shows a rise and then a drop in the preview and in OBS.
- **Alerts overlay**: a third overlay (OBS → Overlay → *Alerts*) shows every follow, sub, gifted sub, tip (bits, KICKs, Super Chats, TikTok and YouTube gifts), raid, channel point reward, TikTok like and share on screen, one card at a time, for Twitch, Kick, YouTube and TikTok together. Settings: theme, position (top / middle / bottom), shape, duration of each alert, sound volume, text size, which alerts to show, platforms and accounts. Motion in the kinetic style: a soft blurred glow in the event colour breathes behind the alert, the words of the title rise one after the other, the user's name is swept by a marker highlight, a small bright dash flicks past, and the icon pops in. Ten styles: **Card** (a glass card floating in 3D, with a burst of sparks and a reflection when it lands), **Big text** (large words right on the scene, no frame, the icon on a small tilted white card), **Impact** (a product shot: the alert falls in as a can label cut in three slices that spin on their own, slam together onto the floor with a splash of droplets and a studio light sweep, and leave with a whip pan) and **Eclipse** (light is the only material: a horizon of light rises into a sunrise, hands over to a ring around the icon, two planes of light cross through it, a crescent sweeps off, then two tall curved walls of light frame the text from the top of the source to the bottom and brighten like an exposure ramp; it all closes like an eyelid), **Glass** (an upright frosted-glass capsule with an ice-white rim; a big soft warm light slides in under it, the capsule morphs into the card, and the light stays on behind the glass near the icon), **Switch** (a soft-UI clay rocker switch rises and clicks on with the chime; hard cut to dark: the switch turns black, light in the event colour floods out from under it, the pill runs out into the alert and a dotted LED track counts down the time left; when it runs out the pill runs back, the switch clicks off and the light dies), **Wave** (one fluid body of thousands of white particles surges behind the icon, its crest crashes over the text and tears into spray; the text forms out of the dust; the body keeps flowing while the alert is on screen and leaves as dust blown away), **Prism** (a cluster of glossy glass bars on a black mirror floor, no card: the camera starts at floor level and cranes up while every chime note kicks the bars higher and sends rings of light across the floor; at rest the camera keeps orbiting and a wave runs through the bars like an equalizer; at the end the words split back into light and the cluster collapses as the camera pulls away), **Silk** (one sphere of very fine lines, cold blue on top and the event colour below, whose sheets slide in and close around the icon; its folds, the flame tongues on top and the ribbon running under the title are the same skin, which keeps deforming while the alert is on screen) and **Neon** (the user's name in chunky extruded chrome letters on a turning ring, lit by neon in front of a red back light: it strikes on the chime and brakes to face the camera; the full name then lights up big in flat neon under it, and the rest of the title below like neon tubes)). Changing the style while an alert is on screen rebuilds it in the new style. Numbers with a thin space ("5 000") never split across lines; long names wrap at their seams (NoxModo / LeRetour) before breaking anywhere. The text scales with the source width, so a narrow source never breaks words. A crystalline glockenspiel chime plays with every alert (built in; volume 0 = silent). The overlay page ignores the system "reduce motion" setting: it is stream output for the viewers, not the streamer's own interface. Like the other overlays, it has one fixed address and follows the dashboard live. A **Send a test alert** button shows a sample alert in the preview and in OBS. Old alerts are never replayed when the source reconnects.

## 1.3.5 — one page per dock

- **One page per OBS dock**: a dock in OBS no longer shows the tabs to the other pages. Each dock (Chat, Community, Stream info, Dashboard) stays on its own page, so your dock lines are not all the same.

## 1.3.0 — faster docks, live overlay settings

Switching tabs in a dock (Chat, Community, Stream info, Dashboard) is about twice as fast when Tramevia Dock runs on a server such as Railway. Measured with 200 ms of network delay: 1.3 s → 0.6 s on average, and the chat 1.1 s → 0.46 s.

- The browser now keeps the dock's scripts, styles and logo between tabs. Their addresses change with every update, so a dock never mixes old and new files.
- A page asks the server for the session, its data and the live connection at the same time, instead of one after the other.
- Overlay: settings are saved in Tramevia Dock and apply live. Chat and Featured message each have one fixed address: paste it into OBS once, then every change in the dashboard shows up on stream right away (paste again only after regenerating the overlay key or changing the port or `PUBLIC_URL`). The first time you open the dashboard, the overlay settings it had remembered in your browser are saved for you. Older overlay addresses (without `&id=`) work exactly as before but don't follow the dashboard; parameters added to a new address still override one setting, for a variant in another scene. Filters now hide the platforms and accounts you untick, so an account connected later shows up by itself.

## 1.2.0 — lighter docks

Docks use far less CPU in OBS, so OBS keeps more for your stream. Measured in Chrome with the demo mode (five live accounts):

| Dock | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Chat, live, quiet chat | 15–20 % of a CPU core | under 1 % |
| Stream info, live | about 17 % | under 0.1 % |
| Chat, busy chat (15 messages per second) | about 35 % | about 6 % |

- The red “live” dot no longer pulses: that animation redrew every dock without a break while you were live.
- New chat messages and events show up without a slide-in animation (the OBS chat overlay keeps its animations).
- The chat list scrolls without redrawing itself, and old messages are removed in batches instead of one by one.
- Overlay: optional small “Chat via Tramevia Dock” credit under the chat (`credit=1`, off by default).
- Overlay builder: says that the settings live in the address, so it must be pasted again into OBS after a change.
- GitHub: issue forms ask for the install type and version, warn against pasting keys, and send questions to Discussions.

## 1.0.0 — first public release

- Multi-account, multi-platform: Twitch (any number of accounts), Kick, YouTube Live, TikTok LIVE (read-only, unofficial).
- Unified multichat dock with emotes (native + 7TV/BTTV/FFZ), badges, replies, moderation, multi-target sending.
- Event feed (follows, subs, gifts, cheers, KICKs, Super Chats, memberships, raids, redemptions, TikTok gifts, YouTube Jewels).
- Community dock: real Twitch chatter lists with roles, active chatters elsewhere, combined viewer count.
- Stream info manager: title / category / tags for every platform at once (Kick no longer allows custom tags), per-platform overrides, presets, diff preview.
- Chat overlay for OBS browser sources (transparent, dark or light cards), configured by URL, with a featured-message mode.
- Setup wizard with exact redirect URIs, connection tests and OBS dock links.
- Local-first (localhost, no password) or cloud (Docker / Railway, password required); encrypted token storage.
- Updates: the dashboard checks GitHub once a day and tells you when a new version is out (`UPDATE_CHECK=0` turns it off). ZIP installs update in one click (or automatically when no account is live, off by default): the download is checked (size + SHA-256), the restart takes a few seconds, never happens while you are live, and the previous version comes back by itself if the new one fails to start. Your `data` folder and `.env` are never touched. Docker, Railway and git installs get a notice with the exact steps.
- Official Docker image `ghcr.io/tramevia/tramevia-dock` (linux/amd64 and linux/arm64, tags `1.0.0`, `1.0`, `1` and `latest`). `compose.yaml` uses it, with an optional nightly auto-update (Watchtower) profile.
- Railway: deploy the official image with Railway's Auto Updates (minor versions and patches) and a `/data` volume. A one-click Railway template is coming right after this release.
- French and English interface and documentation.
